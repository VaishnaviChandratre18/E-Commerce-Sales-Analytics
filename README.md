***E-Commerce Sales Analytics (Python, SQL \& Power BI)***



**Business Problem**



Retail and e-commerce businesses generate large volumes of order data every day, but without structured analysis, it's difficult to identify which products drive revenue, which customers matter most, and how sales fluctuate over time. Without these insights, businesses risk misallocating inventory, missing seasonal trends, and losing visibility into customer retention.



**Project Overview**



This project demonstrates an end-to-end sales analytics workflow on an e-commerce order dataset, combining Python (Pandas, NumPy, Matplotlib, and Seaborn) for data cleaning and exploratory analysis, SQL for business-focused querying, and Power BI for interactive reporting and visualization, to generate insights on product performance, customer behavior, category trends, and monthly revenue patterns.



**Approach**



\- Cleaned and validated the raw sales dataset using Python

\- Calculated key statistics and explored patterns using pandas and NumPy

\- Visualized trends through bar charts, a histogram, a line chart, a boxplot, and a correlation heatmap

\- Answered business questions directly in SQL, covering product performance, customer behavior, and revenue trends

\- Built an interactive Power BI dashboard with KPI cards, a slicer, and multiple visuals to bring the findings together in one view

\- Summarized results into key insights and recommendations



**Tools used**



Python · Pandas · NumPy · Matplotlib · Seaborn · PostgreSQL · Power BI



**Key Insights**



\- Headphones was the top-performing product by revenue, while a few products 

&#x20; consistently underperformed

\- Revenue showed a strong relationship with both quantity sold and price, as expected

\- Revenue was fairly evenly split across all three product categories, with no single 

&#x20; category dominating overall sales

\- Gadgets had the highest average order value among all categories

\- Revenue distribution had small number of high-value orders pulling the average above the typical order value

\- Revenue peaked in February and was lowest in June, showing clear seasonal variation

\- A small number of customers accounted for a large share of total spend, indicating 

&#x20; a loyal but limited customer base.

\- The majority of customers placed more than one order, indicating strong repeat 

&#x20; purchase behavior

\- No missing data or significant outliers were found, indicating a clean and reliable 

&#x20; dataset

\- The Power BI dashboard brings these findings together in one interactive view, 

&#x20; allowing revenue and category performance to be explored in real time



**Recommendations**



\- Prioritize inventory and marketing for consistently top-performing products

\- Investigate and address underperforming products to improve their sales performance

\- Explore what drove the February revenue spike and apply similar strategies to slower months like June

\- Focus on expanding the customer base to reduce revenue concentration among a small group of buyers

\- Introduce a loyalty or repeat-purchase incentive program to further strengthen retention among existing customers

\- Set up regular revenue monitoring to catch seasonal dips earlier and respond proactively
